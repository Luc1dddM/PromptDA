\begin{abstract}
Low-resolution active depth measurements can disambiguate monocular scale, and prompt-conditioned depth foundation models have made their fusion practical at high spatial resolution. Nevertheless, a strong pretrained depth estimator may still make local errors, particularly where visual discontinuities and sparse range observations provide inconsistent evidence. A natural adaptation strategy is to train a small residual head while preserving the pretrained model. Whether an explicitly spatially gated head improves upon a plain residual convolutional network remains an empirical question. We study spatially adaptive consistency-gated refinement (\method), a lightweight adapter for frozen Prompt Depth Anything (\base). A residual branch combines RGB, coarse depth, and intermediate frozen decoder features; a second branch modulates the correction using image--depth gradient agreement, a learned spatial field constructed from LiDAR observation locations, and the coarse estimate. We examine the original objective with an additional boundary-weighted term and a simplified objective that omits it. Under the latter configuration, on 5,599 ARKitScenes validation frames using one training seed, \method\ reduces absolute relative error from 0.011505 to 0.011298 and absolute depth error from 0.013479 to 0.013227\,m compared with frozen \base. It narrowly improves these two metrics over a trained plain CNN, whereas the CNN obtains lower RMSE and RGB-edge-conditioned boundary error. We present a code-faithful formulation, paired comparisons and component studies, and analyze why the current evidence does not yet establish a general architectural advantage. The reported findings concern supervised, frozen-backbone adaptation rather than zero-shot prediction or PromptDA fine-tuning.
\end{abstract}
\vspace{0.6em}
