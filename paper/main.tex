\begin{abstract}
Prompt-conditioned foundation models combine sparse metric measurements with learned visual priors to predict dense depth, yet their estimates may retain spatially nonuniform errors. We investigate whether explicit spatial modulation improves supervised residual adaptation while leaving the pretrained depth model frozen. Spatially Adaptive Consistency-Gated Refinement (SACG) predicts a residual from RGB, coarse Prompt Depth Anything (PromptDA) depth, and frozen decoder features, and modulates it using a learned gate conditioned on normalized RGB--depth gradient agreement, coarse depth, and the spatial support of valid LiDAR observations. We evaluate ARKitScenes (5,599 validation frames) and a local ScanNet++ export (2,000 validation frames per seed), each with three training seeds. In a loss-matched comparison retaining boundary supervision but disabling gate regularization, the gated model reduces mean AbsRel relative to the same residual architecture with a constant gate by 0.70\% on ARKitScenes and 1.03\% on ScanNet++; the gated model is better in all six paired seed-level AbsRel comparisons. A separately selected no-boundary-loss SACG variant improves mean AbsRel over frozen PromptDA by 1.63\% and 0.64\% on the two datasets, respectively. However, a plain residual CNN attains slightly better three-seed mean AbsRel and L1 on ARKitScenes, and it performs better on several other metrics. We therefore attribute the demonstrated architectural benefit specifically to the \emph{controlled learned-gate versus constant-gate comparison}, rather than claiming broad superiority over CNN refinement or geometric boundary preservation. Results are validation-set observations, not independent held-out test evidence.
\end{abstract}
\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent{\small\textbf{Keywords:} metric depth estimation; RGB--LiDAR fusion; depth refinement; frozen foundation models; adaptive gating; depth completion.}
