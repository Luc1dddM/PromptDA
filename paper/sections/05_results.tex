\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}
All results below are aggregates of versioned repository evaluation artifacts, not newly rerun checkpoints. We report three seeds (10, 26, 42) for both datasets. The central architectural question is tested by comparing the same residual network with a learned gate against the constant-gate variant $G\equiv1$, trained from scratch under the same boundary-weighted objective and with gate regularization disabled. Separately, SACG-NoB is the accuracy-selected configuration; the plain CNN is a system-level reference with different input features.

\subsection{Multi-Seed Accuracy of the Selected Refiner}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} gives three-seed mean validation metrics. ARKitScenes uses the same 5,599 evaluation frames for every run. ScanNet++ uses 2,000 frames per run; the frame subset changes with the seed, but all variants within each seed are evaluated on the same subset. Cross-seed means therefore describe the recorded configurations, not a clean estimate of variance from initialization alone.

SACG-NoB reduces mean AbsRel relative to frozen PromptDA by approximately 1.63\% on ARKitScenes and 0.64\% on the local ScanNet++ export; mean L1 reductions are approximately 1.63\% and 1.12\%, respectively. These gains require downstream supervised adaptation and are not zero-shot improvements. The three-seed CNN results also correct a misleading impression from an earlier single-seed comparison: on ARKitScenes, the simple CNN now has slightly lower mean AbsRel and L1 and better RMSE, RGB-edge-conditioned error, and SSIM than SACG-NoB. On ScanNet++, SACG-NoB has marginally lower mean AbsRel and BoundaryAbsRel than the CNN, but the CNN has better mean L1, RMSE, and SSIM. We therefore do not claim general superiority over conventional CNN refinement.

It does not establish which individual cue causes the gain or imply superiority over a separately designed CNN.

\subsection{Loss-Ablation and LiDAR-Field Findings}
The selected SACG-NoB omits the exponentially RGB-edge-weighted boundary term but retains the gradient-consistency cue in its gate and delayed gate regularization. Over the three seeds, ARKitScenes full-objective SACG has mean AbsRel 0.011378 and L1 0.013328, compared with 0.011318 and 0.013259 for SACG-NoB. On ScanNet++, full-objective mean AbsRel and L1 are 0.029128 and 0.048422, compared with 0.029035 and 0.048235 for SACG-NoB. This identifies an empirically useful objective choice, but does not demonstrate that geometric boundaries are unnecessary.

Under the original full-boundary objective, replacing the learned LiDAR-validity field with a fixed field or zeros worsens mean AbsRel on ARKitScenes (learned full SACG: 0.011378; fixed: 0.011397; field removed: 0.011403) and ScanNet++ (learned full SACG: 0.029128; fixed: 0.029195; field removed: 0.029205). Because these field ablations were not conducted under the selected no-boundary objective, their outcome cannot be attributed to the selected configuration. The no-LiDAR-field ablation removes this field from the gate only: frozen PromptDA still consumes the metric LiDAR prompt.

\subsection{Interpreting Boundary and Structural Metrics}
The gate-network ablation improves RGB-edge-conditioned BoundaryAbsRel, but this statistic uses an RGB Sobel edge mask rather than verified ground-truth depth discontinuities. On ScanNet++, SACG-NoB's mean BoundaryAbsRel (0.052222) remains slightly higher than frozen PromptDA's (0.052181), even though it is lower than the CNN's (0.052646). The frozen baseline also has the highest mean auxiliary SSIM on ARKitScenes. Consequently, neither the new gate ablation nor the selected refiner results establish geometric boundary preservation or a general increase in structural fidelity.

\subsection{Attribution and Generalization Limits}
The matched $G\equiv1$ comparison provides evidence for the gate under a shared boundary-weighted objective. It does not isolate the validity field, gradient agreement, and coarse-depth cues individually, or show that the selected SACG-NoB objective has the same gate-specific advantage under its own loss. Validation was used to select objectives and checkpoints. ScanNet++ training/data-selection seeds co-vary, so the three results are not a pure initialization-variance experiment. We have not computed scene-bootstrap confidence intervals, reported independent held-out test results, or measured deployment costs. The supported claim is a consistent benefit of the learned spatial gate in a controlled residual-architecture comparison, while the best overall refiner remains metric- and dataset-dependent.
